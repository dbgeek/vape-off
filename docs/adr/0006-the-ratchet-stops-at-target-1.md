# The Ratchet stops at Target 1

The Ratchet can mechanically write the last Step: `1 - max(1, round(0.1 × 1))` is 0, and nothing in the arithmetic hesitates there. We stop it anyway. Every Target from the Baseline's conclusion down to 1 says *vape less than this*; Target 0 says *stop*, which is a different kind of sentence, and having it arrive on a cadence of the app's own choosing makes the app the author of the day you quit. So the Ratchet's last Earned Step is the one that reaches Target 1, and the `1 → 0` Step is **Declared** — offered by the app once you have held Target 1 for five of seven Logical Days, and written by your tap.

The same boundary works in reverse. `0 → 1` is Declared too, available while Target 0 is in force, and it is the only raise anywhere in the programme. Without it the design has a hole precisely where it claims a principle: at Target 0 a sustained relapse leaves every day not Met, Momentum on the floor, Steps Remaining and the Quit Horizon both retired, and the app with nothing left to say — while someone who simply stops logging goes Unknown and is untouched, because [ADR 0001](./0001-unlogged-days-are-unknown-not-zero.md) holds Momentum across gaps. Honest logging would carry a penalty that silence does not, which is the one incentive this design refuses everywhere else.

That the Ratchet still never raises the Target is therefore literally true, and the word keeps meaning what it says. A Ratchet Step is Earned or Declared; only the Ratchet writes the first kind, only you write the second, and the second exists at one boundary and nowhere else.

## Considered options

- **Floor at Target 1, with an earned offer** (chosen). The Ratchet's stated job is to test whether you can hold a Target, and holding 1 is evidence about 1 — the jump from *one puff* to *none* is where quitting actually lives, and it is not the same test. A hard floor with no offer was rejected for leaving the app mute at the culmination of the whole programme, which matters more than usual here: nothing on the device can fire a notification ([#2](https://github.com/dbgeek/vape-off/issues/2)), so an offer the app declines to surface is one that may never be seen.
- **Let the Ratchet write `1 → 0`.** What [ADR 0002](./0002-the-quit-horizon-simulates-the-ratchet.md) assumed. Rejected: besides authorship, it changes the rules underneath you without asking — at Target 0 only a Clear Day can be Met, so the Ratchet would be making a daily declaration compulsory as a side effect of an arithmetic step.
- **A free choice of Target on the way back up.** Rejected: a Target you can set is not a Target. One number you may raise to, and only from zero, keeps the raise un-gameable — Target 1 is barely a concession, so there is nothing to be won by taking it dishonestly.
- **Re-measure after a collapse** — a second Baseline setting a fresh Target from recent counts. Rejected for v1 and recorded as out of scope. `Baseline` is defined as the *first* seven Known Logical Days and `Baseline Average` is frozen at its close; a second one reopens both, and it is a subsystem for a case a user who has not started yet may never reach.
- **Never raise at all.** The status quo. Rejected as above: the mechanism is dead for exactly the person who most needs it, and it charges them for logging.

## Consequences

- **A full collapse has no in-app recovery, and this is accepted.** Target 1 is no use to someone back at twelve puffs a day; for them the app has only the honest reading and no route. Revisit if it happens; do not build for it first.
- **Target 0 is a state you live in, not an ending.** There is no graduation screen — an app that declares victory has nothing to offer on the day after, which is the day it is most needed. Steps Remaining and the Quit Horizon retire there, as [ADR 0002](./0002-the-quit-horizon-simulates-the-ratchet.md) anticipated, and **Longest Gap** takes the headline: it is the one reading in the glossary that only becomes interesting at zero.
- **The Ratchet is dormant at Target 0, not finished.** A Declared `0 → 1` puts Target 1 back in force and the ordinary five-of-seven rule resumes, so the app can offer the handover again. Both directions land in the same append-only log, which means a wobble is *visible* in the record rather than erased, and the descent already walked is never invalidated.
- **Step Cadence counts Earned Steps only.** It estimates the rate of the mechanism, and a Declared Step is timed by a decision of yours; counting a `0 → 1` would record a backwards move as an interval of descent. This composes with ADR 0002's stall rule without a special case: after a relapse the open interval since the last Earned Step swallows the relapse, exceeds twice the cadence, and the Quit Horizon withdraws itself exactly when it should.
- **Steps Remaining counts the Declared handover**, so it reads to the end of the programme rather than to the end of the Ratchet's part in it. Anything else would understate the distance left by one at the only point where the user can count the remainder themselves.
- **The two Declared acts do not share a home.** The handover offer belongs on the logging screen — earned, rare, and worth seeing. The step-back belongs in Stats, behind a deliberate trip: a standing *raise my Target* control on the screen you open twenty times a day is a give-up button, and the app should never be the one suggesting it. The asymmetry matches the authorship — the app surfaces what it has evidence for, and only you have evidence that you are not holding zero.
- **The single-puff tail stands.** From Target 14 down, every Step is one puff — a constant fourteen Steps regardless of Baseline, and for a Baseline Average of 20 that is fourteen of the sixteen Steps in the whole programme. The relative bite grows as it descends (3 → 2 is a third, 2 → 1 a half), which is the right shape: the hardest cuts get the most time. Accelerating the bottom would re-cost [#4](https://github.com/dbgeek/vape-off/issues/4) and ADR 0002's calibration to save a few weeks at the one place in the programme where haste is least useful.
